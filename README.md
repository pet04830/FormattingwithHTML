  <section>
    <h2>Introduction and Purpose</h2>

   <p>Within my English classes over the years, Maya Angelou has been a recurring name throughout the texts we had to read. I found myself being drawn to her writing because she speaks for those who cannot, giving them a platform. She writes for those who, as she states in her text, are “caged birds.” These caged birds symbolize African Americans who have been discriminated against and silenced throughout history. This is the central topic of her poem <em>“Caged Bird”</em>, which is connected to her autobiography <em>I Know Why the Caged Bird Sings</em>.</p>

   <p><strong>The poem's main purpose is to give a voice to African Americans while showing the contrast between freedom and oppression.</strong> Angelou emphasizes the power of the human spirit and how even a caged bird, who should have no hope, perseveres and continues to sing. Through metaphors and other forms of figurative language, she addresses racial injustice and brings it into public discussion. This direct message of visibility and strength creates a powerful bond between Angelou and her readers while challenging them to recognize oppression.</p>

   <p>Angelou uses <em>metaphor, allusion, and repetition</em> to underscore the physiological and emotional effects of racial discrimination while empowering African Americans by giving voice to their struggle and highlighting the human spirit's resilience.</p>
   </section>

   <section>
     <h2>Audience and Message</h2>

   <p>The poem speaks directly to African Americans who have been oppressed or who have experienced the feeling of being trapped in society. Angelou shows how she relates to this audience as an African American and gives them hope throughout the text. By doing so, Angelou validates the lived experiences of her primary audience and reclaims space for African American voices in America.</p>

   <p>While reading, I see myself as a secondary audience because I am reading to gain awareness about the subject and to learn more about experiences outside my own. As a secondary reader, I feel Angelou is showing the emotional and psychological impacts that many people have faced because of oppression. She conveys this message through imagery such as the “grave of dreams,” which can represent denied opportunities that have been passed down through generations.</p>

   <blockquote>“But a caged bird stands on the grave of dreams / His shadow shouts on a nightmare scream”</blockquote>

   <p>These lines create a direct connection to African Americans because Angelou uses the image of confinement to represent experiences of discrimination. The poem also speaks indirectly to people who live with privilege or without awareness of oppression. The free bird can be viewed as a metaphorical mirror for these readers, reflecting their privilege and encouraging empathy and self-reflection.</p>
   </section>

   <section>
     <h2>Rhetorical Strategies</h2>

   <p>The main rhetorical strategies used within the poem are <em>allusion, repetition, and metaphor</em>. Each strategy strengthens Angelou's message and helps connect the poem with her intended audience.</p>

   <ul>
     <li><strong>Allusion:</strong> The caged bird and its experiences indirectly represent the experiences of African Americans without repeatedly naming the group.</li>
     <li><strong>Metaphor:</strong> The contrast between the caged bird and the free bird represents the difference between oppression and freedom.</li>
     <li><strong>Repetition:</strong> The repeated idea that the caged bird continues to sing reinforces hope and creates an emotional rhythm throughout the poem.</li>
   </ul>

   <p>Angelou's use of metaphor strengthens the heaviness of her message because readers can connect the bird's experience with the feeling of being trapped and trying to find hope in a seemingly hopeless world. Many African Americans have experienced these feelings firsthand, so the metaphors can speak directly to their experiences. The reader becomes emotionally connected to the poem and can recognize the connection between confinement and the desire for freedom.</p>

   <p>Repetition also holds strong power because the ideas are continually reinforced while reading. The caged bird's continued singing becomes a symbol of hope. Angelou highlights the struggles African Americans face while also showing how persistent the human spirit can be. The poem's relatively simple and direct language allows it to reach a wide audience, helping Angelou achieve her goal of public expression.</p>
   </section>

   <section>
     <h2>Impact and Conclusion</h2>

   <p>Within Maya Angelou's poem, she uses metaphors, allusion, and repetition to underscore the physiological and emotional effects of racial discrimination while empowering African Americans by giving voice to their struggle and highlighting the human spirit's resilience. I believe this text is a strong example of public writing because of both the way it is taught in many schools and the lasting impact it can have on readers.</p>

   <p>The poem raises awareness about social justice and what African Americans have experienced on emotional and political levels. Angelou inspires readers while also asking them to consider a better world where the caged bird can be free. Her rhetorical language is one of the strongest elements because readers can feel the emotional force behind her words. There is so much to examine in the text that it becomes a strong example of rhetorical writing.</p>

   <p>Most importantly, the poem succeeds because it brings an unseen perspective into the light by taking private suffering and turning it into a discussion about justice and freedom. Oppression continues to affect communities in different ways, which allows the poem's message to connect with readers across backgrounds. By blending emotional truth with rhetoric, Angelou's poem continues to encourage reflection, empathy, and hope.</p>
